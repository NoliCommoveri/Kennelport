# KennelPort: what breeder websites look like (visual survey)

> Status: observations, not a design. Home pages in sections 1–9, inner pages in
> section 10. What real breeder websites look like on screen:
> layout, photos, colour, type, borders, spacing, and which elements are there or
> missing. The companion to [`KennelPort_Site_Directory.md`](KennelPort_Site_Directory.md),
> which covers what the pages say.

## 1. How this was done

- 28 breeder home pages and 8 inner pages (a litter page, planned litters, available
  puppies, two dog pages, a litters-and-planning page) opened in a real browser
  (Chromium) at desktop width (1366px) and phone width (390px), scrolled so lazy images
  loaded, and captured as full-page screenshots.
- Fonts, colours, button corners and page heights were read from the rendered page.
- 26 sites rendered. Two (Doodle Patch Farm, Rosehaven Homestead) show a "Confirm you
  are human" check before the home page, so a visitor's first sight is a bot check.
- Breeds: goldens, labs, doodles of several kinds, Aussies, collies and sheepdogs,
  dachshunds, German Shepherds, Malinois, Boerboels, Lagottos, Bernese.

**What they're built with:** Wix 7, WordPress 7, Squarespace 4, Weebly 2, Showit 1;
custom or hand-built 5 (one from 2000s-era HTML, one from a breeder-site template
service, three recent custom builds).

## 2. Five looks

The 26 sites fall into five recognisable looks. Most sites are the second.

**1. Hand-built, early web (5: Ryan's Goldens, Angels of Gold, Sweetheart Goldens, Windy
G Farm, Endless Mountain Labradors).**
- Narrow centred column on a patterned or photo background (navy satin texture, a red
  barn photo, a blurred landscape); yellow or pink page colours.
- Navigation as a **left sidebar of stacked image buttons**, one per page or litter
  (Angels of Gold has 25: "JOSIE X MONTE PUPPIES 10/22/21", …), or tab-shaped buttons.
- Times or Trebuchet text, small; WordArt-style logos; bold blue warning text.
- Badge and logo walls (Facebook, YouTube, credit cards, AKC Reunite, "Stop puppy mills"),
  supplement adverts with order codes, "This site was last updated 05/06/22", "always
  under construction".
- Ryan's Goldens isn't mobile-ready at all: the phone shows the desktop page cut off.

**2. DIY site-builder template, home-made (12: Gardner Lane, Sunflower Dachshunds, Simply
Goldendoodles, Stormie's Doodles, McClelland Doodles, Vom Geliebten Haus, Fine and Dandy,
Pikes Peak Doodles, Kodalihart, Shepherd Pals, Goldens On The Rocks, Hunter's
Goldstrike).**
- A full-width photo across the top, then alternating bands of a theme colour.
- **One strong colour per site**, often personal rather than tasteful: blush pink and
  tan; sage green with purple buttons; royal blue and cyan; dark taupe and tan; maroon,
  magenta and brown; yellow and white; dusty pink with plum.
- Decorative type for the name: script logos, Lobster-style italics, a cartoon marker
  font (Stormie's), a hand-scribble font (Kodalihart).
- **Made graphics**: Canva banners with a photo strip and sunflowers, a heart-shaped
  photo collage on floral wallpaper, an inspirational quote image, promo cards for a
  litter ("RILEY RYE × MAVERICK"), a credentials checklist as an image.
- Odd photo shapes: octagons, ovals, diamonds, tilted frames with drop shadows.
- **Large empty areas** where a slideshow, gallery or animation didn't load: big blank
  magenta, yellow, brown or white blocks are common, on phones too.
- Text often tiny, light-weight and low-contrast (grey 12px Poppins on pink; tan on brown;
  justified italic paragraphs).

**3. Polished small-breeder template (5: Blackwood Acres, Field Bred Golden Retrievers,
Golden Haven, Foreverly Kennel, Lonestar Doodles).**
- Cream, beige or white backgrounds with earthy accents: forest green, ochre, rust,
  olive, terracotta, teal.
- **Serif headings** (Garamond, Sansita, Cormorant, Playfair-style) over a clean sans
  body (Josefin, Nunito, Hanken Grotesk); small letter-spaced caps "eyebrow" labels above
  headings ("OUR PROGRAM", "THE DOGS BEHIND FBGR").
- Photo and text side by side, alternating; generous white space; square-cornered
  buttons in a dark accent colour; thin rules as dividers.
- A real family photo of the breeders, outdoors, in most of them.
- Even here, two had blank bands where content failed to load.

**4. Editorial, agency-built (1: Northwest Lagotto).**
- Off-white with forest green and muted gold; large Cormorant serif headings with an
  italic gold second line ("A Lagotto Romagnolo Breeder / *Raising Puppies for Your
  Family*").
- Long-form writing, almost no puppy photos when there's no litter; a dark photo hero;
  a green **trust strip** of five small icon-and-caps items under the hero (AKC
  registered, club member, Puppy Culture, health testing, town).
- Numbered cards (01 / 02 / 03), a breed "at a glance" facts table in a bordered box, a
  single large italic testimonial with "Rated 5.0 on Google · 18 family reviews".
- On phones, a **sticky bottom bar**: "No litter now. Next: Lottie + Louis, puppies
  expected this winter · Join the waitlist".

**5. Commercial, shop-like (3: Crockett Doodles, Bernedoodles & Co, Peterbuilt
Boerboel).**
- A thin coloured utility bar on top (delivery, phone, Instagram), a logo and a bright
  **Apply** or **Reserve** button.
- Puppies shown as **product cards**: studio photo, name, breed line, price; sale prices;
  a cart; "Reserve your pup".
- Promotions ("$200 OFF ANY PUPPY plus EXTRA $100 OFF first 15 families"), BBB rating,
  comparison tables (us vs others with green, yellow and red dots), counters ("500+ dogs
  sold").
- Peterbuilt is closest to a web app: rounded white cards with soft shadows, pill
  badges, a **Login** button, a "Last updated 4 weeks ago" pill with a green dot, a
  carousel strip announcing the next pairing with round parent photos.

## 3. Elements, one by one

### Top of the page

- **Hero:** a full-width photo with the kennel name or a line over it on about 17 of 26; the rest open with a graphic, a collage, a photo beside text or a plain colour band.
  The photo is usually a close-up of one dog or a puppy pile; sometimes the breeder with
  a dog; sometimes action (a golden retrieving a duck through water). Two heroes were plain
  colour bands (the image missing or never set).
- **Logo:** most have one: a dog silhouette, a paw, a script name, a circular badge with
  an illustration. Several are photos or Canva graphics rather than real logos.
- **Navigation:** a horizontal menu of 5 to 9 items on most, often in small caps, with
  dropdowns; on phones a hamburger menu. The old sites use a sidebar. Menus with 15 or
  more items wrap onto two lines or scatter (Foreverly's menu floats around an oval
  "Contact Us" button).
- **A call to action in the header:** "Apply", "Join the priority list", "Adopt a puppy",
  "Contact us" on about a third.
- **A banner above the menu:** "Golden Retriever applications are open →", "We deliver
  nationwide", a scrolling marquee "We have summer 2026 puppies available".

### Dogs

- **Grids of photos, captioned with very little.** Field Bred: square outdoor photos,
  then the call name, a title suffix (SH, JH, MH) and "DAM" / "SIRE" / "FUTURE SIRE" in
  small grey caps. Peterbuilt: a white card with the photo and the registered name only.
  McClelland: oval photos with a thin white border, then the name in serif and an italic
  block (generation, weight, birth date, "Genetically tested through Embark click here",
  parents, "Cardiac cleared by vet").
- **Split into girls and boys**, as headed sections ("Our Girls", "Our Boys") rather than
  symbols. No site used ♂ / ♀ symbols.
- **Photo shapes vary:** square (most common), portrait, oval, circle (parents on litter
  cards), frames with borders and shadows (older sites).
- **Health results** are mostly a text list, a link to Embark, or **printed onto a photo
  as a poster** ("OFA Elbows 0/0 · OFA Hips: Mild · Embark DNA: Clear" across a dog's
  picture). No site showed health results as a table.

### Litters and puppies

Four ways of showing puppies came up:
1. **A text block with the litter's facts** (Gardner Lane, Hunter's Goldstrike): "Bailey's
   puppies are 7 weeks old!", born, ready to leave, "3 boys", "These puppies have been
   adopted!"; planned pairings as coloured cards with due date, take-home date, price and
   "(UPDATED 9/4/2026)".
2. **Photo pairs with a one-line caption** (Windy G Farm): two photos per puppy side by
   side, then `"Tuscan" Black tri female RESERVED Thank you Jerrad from OKC`, with
   RESERVED in red and Available in blue, separated by large gaps.
3. **A product grid** (Lonestar, Bernedoodles & Co): 3-up portrait photos, every puppy
   staged the same way (Lonestar puts each one on the same small white armchair outdoors
   with a bandana), then name ("Pink Bernedoodle Girl"), a grey line ("Bernedoodle ·
   Female · Medium · F1") and the price, sometimes with an old price struck through.
4. **A detailed card per puppy** (Peterbuilt): a photo carousel with arrows and a "1 / 4"
   counter, an orange "Reserved" corner badge, the collar name, sex, price in green,
   a note, **temperament sliders** (Dominant–Passive, Active–Lazy, Vocal–Quiet,
   Small–Big, "Show all 11 attributes") and a line saying these are early impressions,
   not guarantees. Above the puppies, a litter card with small round photos of the four
   grandparents over larger round photos of the two parents, and pills "Born May 27,
   2026 · 22 weeks old".

**Planned litters** were shown as:
- coloured cards per pairing (Gardner Lane);
- a white card with round sire and dam photos joined by "×", pill badges (registry,
  "Expected: December 2026"), a write-up and **two columns of picks** (male, female),
  each row "Pick 1 $5,000" with an amber Reserved or green Available pill (Peterbuilt);
- a status card with a green header strip "26 OCTOBER 2026 · AWAITING CONFIRMATION", a
  3-cell table (breeding date, outcome, puppies expected) and an indented update note,
  plus small cards "ANTICIPATED · SPRING 2027" for later pairings (Northwest Lagotto).

### Trust

- **Badges** on more than half: Good Dog, Bred with H.E.A.R.T., OFA, Embark, PawPrint,
  Orivet, Puppy Culture, AKC Breeder of Merit, GANA Gold, BBB. Shown as a row of logos,
  in a yellow box, or greyed out in a footer strip.
- **Google reviews:** a floating "5.0 ★ 33 reviews" badge, a card with stars and one
  quote, a trust bar "★ 4.8 · 108 Google reviews · Est. 2010 · Health guarantee ·
  Weatherford, Texas" with small icons.
- **Testimonials** in five forms: plain two-column quotes with no photos (Blackwood);
  bordered cards with gold stars, a first name and "10 months ago · Google review ↗"
  (Lonestar); round avatar photo, italic text, name (Endless Mountain, Pikes Peak); one
  large italic quote centred (Northwest Lagotto); a carousel with an Instagram collage
  (Bernedoodles & Co).
- **The breeders themselves:** a family photo outdoors, the breeder holding puppies, or a
  framed portrait of the couple, on most sites.

### Process and extras

- **Numbered steps:** three icon steps (fill out application, place your deposit,
  prepare for puppy); six steps in a grid each with its own button; four numbered steps
  on a line (Apply, Place Hold, Choose, Bring Home); a "Your puppy's timeline" with
  numbered circles and cartoon illustrations by week.
- **"What comes home with your puppy"** as an icon row (blanket, food bag, contract,
  guarantee, vaccination record, leash) or a two-column checklist with green ticks.
- **Video:** native players, YouTube embeds, Instagram video grids with a "VIDEO" tag and
  dates.

### Bottom of the page

- Footers range from one line to 4 to 6 columns of links (a sitemap), a dark background
  in a site colour, social icons, an email sign-up form ("Join the priority list",
  "Stay in the loop"), opening hours, "Visits by appointment only".
- Australian sites show **registration numbers** (Dogs Victoria member number, Pet
  Exchange Register number) because the state requires it.

## 4. Colour, type, borders, spacing

- **Colour:** earthy is the most common family (cream, beige, forest green, ochre, rust,
  brown, olive) on about 9 sites; pastel or pink on about 5; one bold personal colour on
  about 6 (royal blue, magenta, sage, yellow, dark taupe, maroon); dark backgrounds on 3
  (navy satin texture, navy and gold, dark taupe). Pure white with a single accent on
  about 4.
- **Type:** serif headings with sans body text is the most common pairing. Script or
  hand-lettered type appears on about half, nearly always for the kennel name or one
  accent word. Body sizes measured from 10px to 20px; several sites have body text at
  10–12px, light weight, low contrast, which is hard to read on a phone.
- **Buttons:** square corners on about half, fully rounded pills on about a third, small
  rounding (6–15px) on the rest. Outline buttons on dark photos; solid accent buttons
  elsewhere.
- **Borders and shadows:** old sites frame everything (thick black rounded photo frames,
  bordered content columns, tab-shaped boxes). Builder templates use soft drop shadows
  on tilted photos. Polished templates use almost none: hairline rules and flat colour
  bands. The commercial sites use white cards with soft shadows and rounded corners.
- **Section dividers:** flat colour bands are most common; wave and arc shapes on four
  (Gardner Lane, Stormie's, Field Bred's arched "Our Girls" header); photo strips (a
  floral fabric photo, a fern photo) on two.
- **Spacing:** old sites are cramped (small text, little padding). Builder templates swing
  between generous padding and empty blocks hundreds of pixels tall. Polished and
  editorial sites keep wide, even spacing.
- **Length:** home pages are long. Desktop height measured from about 2,000px to 19,700px,
  typically 5,000–9,000px: four to ten screens of scrolling on a laptop, much more on a
  phone.

## 5. On a phone

- Every builder-made site reflowed to one column with a hamburger menu; the one
  hand-built 2000s site didn't.
- Photos stack full width; three- and four-up grids become one or two up.
- Heroes shrink: text over photos gets small or collides with the subject.
- Long text walls get longer: paragraphs that are a block on desktop become several
  screens on a phone.
- Extras that appear only on phones: a sticky waitlist bar (Northwest Lagotto), a
  newsletter pop-up covering the page (Field Bred), a Wix "Join us on the App" banner, a
  floating chat bubble, reCAPTCHA badges.

## 6. Photos

- **Candid phone photos are the norm:** puppies on blankets, dogs in yards, kids hugging
  puppies, people in orange vests at a hunt test (a 3×3 grid of event snapshots on
  Hunter's Goldstrike). Lighting, crop and aspect ratio vary photo to photo.
- **Staged sets** on a few: the same white armchair for every puppy, studio white
  backgrounds, colour-matched bandanas and bows.
- **Made images** on many: Canva posters with a dog's name in huge letters, sepia
  composites with lightning, collages with paw borders, an AI-looking illustration banner.
- Photos are the main content on most sites. Dog pages and litter pages are mostly
  pictures with a line of text.

## 7. Present and absent

**Nearly everywhere:** a big photo at the top; dog photos with names; puppies or "next
litter" near the top of the home page; the breeder's own photo; a way to apply; social
links; some kind of trust badge or review.

**Common:** numbered steps; a "what comes home with your puppy" list; video; an email
sign-up; badges in the footer.

**Rare:** prices on puppy cards (the commercial sites and a few small ones); structured
health results; a "last updated" date (four sites); a breed facts table
(one); temperament sliders (one); pick tables on planned litters (one); a status timeline
for a planned breeding (one); a family login (one, plus a Login button on another).

**Absent everywhere:** sex symbols; breadcrumbs (except one inner page); search, apart
from a few icons; a privacy page link on most; accessible contrast on many.

## 8. Your Cotton Paws example, next to the real sites

The example looks like the **polished template look (2) with the commercial site's
content density (5)**: cream background, serif headings with a script accent word, earthy
brown buttons, a full-width photo hero, an "Our Story" block with three icon features,
dog cards, current and upcoming litter cards with round parent photos joined by "×",
photo testimonials with stars, an inline waitlist form, a dark footer.

What it shares with real sites: nearly every element in it exists somewhere in the survey.
The upcoming-litter card with round parent photos and "×" matches Peterbuilt's; the
testimonial with photo, stars and family name matches Pikes Peak and Endless Mountain;
the icon trio matches Pikes Peak and Stormie's; the cream and brown palette matches
Blackwood and Golden Haven.

Where real sites differ from it:
- **Photos.** Its photos are all the same light, crop and quality. Real sites mix phone
  photos, staged shots and Canva graphics in every aspect ratio. Dog cards in a real grid
  rarely line up that evenly without cropping each photo to the same shape.
- **Density.** It fits dogs, two current litters, two planned pairings, testimonials and a
  waitlist form into about two screens. Real home pages spread far less across five to
  ten screens, with more photos and more text per section.
- **Dog cards.** Real sites caption dogs with a name, a role (dam, sire) and sometimes a
  title, and split girls and boys into sections. None used ♂ / ♀ symbols or a short bio
  on the card; bios live on the dog's own page.
- **Litter cards.** Real litter cards carry dates and counts ("Born August 14", "2 males
  and 1 female available"), as the example does, but often also a price, a ready date,
  "updated" stamps and status colours.
- **Trust.** The example has no badges, no Google rating, no "last updated", no
  credentials; most real sites have at least one.

## 9. What a site template has to cope with

These are facts the survey shows any template will meet, not style choices:
- **Uneven photos:** every aspect ratio and quality, so cards need a fixed crop with a
  chosen focus point, and pages must still look right with one photo or none.
- **Empty states:** no litter on the ground is normal ("No litter now. Next: …").
- **Long names and long text:** registered names, 300-word litter write-ups, 20-item
  menus.
- **Graphics with text in them:** breeders upload posters with names and health results
  printed on the image, which screen readers and search engines can't read; the plan
  already asks for health results as data and alt text on every photo.
- **Phones first:** most of this is read on phones, where the failures above (tiny light
  text, blank blocks, pop-ups, a desktop-only site) were worst.
- **Privacy slips seen in the wild:** a buyer's first name and town next to a reserved
  puppy (Windy G Farm), a street address in the header (Sweetheart Goldens). KennelPort's
  rules (no buyers, town and region only) already prevent both.

## 10. Inner pages

A second pass captured 39 inner pages from 18 of the sites, at both widths: dog listings,
litter and available-puppy pages, reviews, about pages, how-it-works pages, an FAQ, a
health guarantee and a contract page. The home page sets the look; these pages are where
visitors spend their time, and where the differences between sites are largest.

### What inner pages share

- **A title band at the top of almost every inner page:** a full-width strip in a dark or
  accent colour (charcoal, rust, navy, forest green), or a darkened photo, with the page
  name large and centred ("Our Golden Retrievers", "Testimonials", "How to Adopt a
  Puppy"), sometimes a one-line subtitle. About 15–30% of the first screen.
- **The same header and footer as the home page**, so the footer's sign-up form, badges
  and links repeat on every page.
- **One long page per section.** Every dog, every litter, every review on one scrolling
  page. Only the shop-like sites and one Wix site gave dogs their own pages; most sites
  have no page for an individual dog or puppy.

### Dog listings (11 pages)

Five layouts:
1. **One row per dog, photo beside facts** (Blackwood, Simply Goldendoodles): the
   registered name as a heading, then a list with bold labels (Call name, Breed, AKC #,
   Date of birth, Size in lbs, PennHIP, OFA elbows, DNA: genetically clear), then a
   4–6 line bio; one large portrait photo on the other side, or a main photo with a strip
   of thumbnails underneath. On a phone: photo, name, list, bio.
2. **A grid of cards** (Gardner Lane, Fine and Dandy, Peterbuilt): square photo, name,
   a short centred paragraph, a "Read More" or "About Me" button. Peterbuilt's cards add
   "Hips: OFA Good" and coloured rating bars (Overall health 8.0, Conformation, Dog
   friendly, Kid friendly, …, "+4 more attributes"); cards end up different heights.
3. **A photo grid with names only** (Field Bred, Endless Mountain, Sunflower): square
   photos with the name and "DAM" / "SIRE" under each, or names watermarked onto the
   photos, or a white caption box with the title ("AKC Conformation Grand Champion").
   Endless Mountain also has a second grid of "past mamas".
4. **Canva poster per dog** (Hunter's Goldstrike): a collage of three or four photos of
   the dog with its call name in big serif capitals and the kennel logo, and a pill
   button with the registered name. No facts on the page.
5. **Show-world data panels** (Vom Geliebten Haus studs): a black box per dog, side photo
   in show stance, a cream panel with the registered name and titles underlined ("SG
   NESTO vom Haus Tchorz, BH, IPO1 Kkl"), hip and elbow scores, DNA, sire and dam with
   their titles, coat type, owners, and "Stud service: to approved females, live, fresh
   or frozen" in red.

Also seen: a dogs page with **no names at all**, just two slideshows ("find out more
about each mother by clicking each photo", Pikes Peak); dogs split under "Studs" and
"Mamas" or "Our Boys" and "Our Girls"; one page ending in about 1,000px of empty colour.

### Litters and available puppies (8 pages)

- **A litter as a block of centred facts** (Blackwood, Hunter's Goldstrike): a named
  litter ('"Chronicles of Narnia" Litter') or a pair ("Bettee x Maverick") as the heading;
  date of birth, mom, dad, "3 boys, 2 girls", adoption day or go-home date, "All puppies
  are spoken for", "Priced at $3500 limited registration"; one photo of the dam nursing,
  or a 3×2 grid of the week's puppy photos; an **Apply** button. Hunter's adds a weekly
  update paragraph ("These photos show the puppies at five weeks old. The pink blanket
  shows the three girls…").
- **Planned litters as cards** (McClelland, Hunter's): dam and sire photos side by side in
  one frame, "Kai & Champ's 'Rudolph' Litter", the cross in italics, a size class
  ("Medium Goldendoodles"), then Status, Due date, Go home, and **Anticipating**: colours,
  coat, adult size. Above them, small-print disclaimers (heat cycles are estimates; we
  keep the first two picks).
- **Puppies as shop products** (Bernedoodles & Co, 40+ items): breadcrumb, a category
  sidebar, 3-up studio photos, "★ Pixie – Micro Munchkin Bernedoodle Girl", price in the
  accent colour, "Sale!" badges, struck-through prices, "6 Month Old –" for older pups.
  Future litters are sold as products too, each with a themed graphic ("Cavapoo Litter
  for Valentines Day ♥").
- **Puppies as posters** (Stormie's): each puppy's card is a Canva poster (the puppy in a
  costume and crown in a castle scene, "Your Little Prince Awaits", a price badge, icons
  for weight and dates) beside a paragraph and "PRICE: $1600 · 7% Florida sales tax ·
  Total: $1712". The facts live inside the image.
- **Parents first, then puppies** (Simply Goldendoodles): "Fall litter coming!", the two
  parents' portraits labelled Dam and Sire with bios, then a "Puppies" section with **two
  empty black placeholder boxes** left over from the template.
- **Upcoming as dates and counts** (Crockett): "November 13–15 · 4 puppies may still be
  available" in columns, with the details behind links; a widget area that didn't load.

### Reviews and testimonials (7 pages)

- **Plain quotes in columns** (Blackwood): three-column masonry of text-only quotes,
  "— Olson family", no photos, stars or dates; some quotes are 200 words.
- **Rounded review cards** (Peterbuilt): a navy band with "4.9 ★★★★★ 11 Google ratings", a
  note saying the reviews come from the Google Business Profile, then two columns of
  white cards: reviewer name, stars, quote.
- **Gradient cards** (Stormie's): magenta cards with white text, stars and a first name;
  buttons out to Facebook and Google reviews.
- **Quote beside photo** (Shepherd Pals): alternating rows, a grey quote box (a bold
  italic headline line, the quote, first name and initial, town and state) beside a
  photo of the family with their grown dog, captioned with the dog's name and its litter
  ("Classie Flossy – Lassie's 12/31/25 litter"). A visitor review form sits at the top;
  "Reviews (42) ★★★★★".
- **Reviews as blog posts** (Gardner Lane): a 4-up grid of puppy photos, each with a date
  and a headline ("They Shared Pictures of our Puppy Regularly!") and "Read More".
- **Curated and filterable** (Northwest Lagotto): a dark hero ("What Happens After the
  Puppy Comes Home") with counts, "Curated by Mark Nelson · Published with family
  permission · Updated 2026"; three question-led quote cards; a featured quote on a green
  band; a "Verified on Google" grid; filter tabs (All families / The experience / Puppy
  Culture / The dog); each quote signed with first names, town, dog and year.
- **A widget that never loaded** (Crockett): the heading "Recent reviews" followed by
  blank space.

### About pages (5 pages)

- **The family as a team, one row per person** (Blackwood: "The Blackwood Acres Crew";
  Hunter's: "Meet the Family"): a portrait of each family member with a dog, including
  the children, each with a job title ("Raleigh || puppy teacher, dog groomer", "Essex ||
  puppy snuggle buddy", "Dog Chore Minions") and a paragraph.
- **A row of tall portraits as the hero** (Lonestar): five professional photos of family
  members with puppies side by side, then short centred sections divided by hairlines.
- **Founder story with a fact panel** (Northwest Lagotto): a portrait card with a small
  table (location, property, AKC status, program, club), a "by the numbers" panel, a
  YouTube video, a trust strip of six badges, philosophy as four numbered cards.

### How it works and policies (8 pages)

- **Numbered steps as headings with text** (Blackwood, 7 steps in two columns; McClelland,
  6 steps as a long document with bold sub-labels such as "Before joining a pick list",
  "Virtual selection").
- **Numbered cards** (Field Bred): six cards (01 Submit the request form … 06 Go-home
  preparation), then a dark band with **"2027 Puppy Pricing: $3,600 females, $3,300
  males"**, a checklist "What every puppy receives", and side boxes for payment methods
  and pickup. At the top, a **status box: "Reservations currently paused · Our
  reservation list is currently full"** with a button to the interest list.
- **Small facts tables and diagrams** (Lonestar): "Two ways to adopt" as two bordered
  boxes; Deposit › Details › Pickup with arrows; a four-cell table (Deposit $300 · Goes
  toward total · Refundable: No · Good for an entire year); pick order drawn as "1st ›
  2nd › 3rd › and so on"; accordions; a timeline bar (eyes open, photos, ready); an FAQ
  accordion.
- **A vertical timeline with icons** (Stormie's): ten steps alternating left and right of
  a line (Litter announced, Application, $200 deposit, Breeding, Litter dates sent,
  Puppies born, Weekly photos, Pick day at 6 weeks, Final balance at 7 weeks, Go home at
  8 weeks), then "Sale terms" with emoji headings.
- **The application pasted into the page** (Shepherd Pals): 20 numbered questions to copy
  into an email, with warranty terms in bold capitals inside question 13, and "We hope to
  have more pups in the fall of 2024" still at the top.
- **FAQ as cards with search** (Peterbuilt): a search box, then one white card per
  question with a link icon, "Answered by breeder Derek Peterson · Updated September
  2026".
- **Health guarantee as a long document** (Bernedoodles & Co): uppercase headings (what it
  covers, what it doesn't, if a condition is diagnosed, lifetime take-back, microchip and
  shelter policy), an effective date, one photo.

### What the inner pages add to the picture

- **Individual dog and puppy pages are rare.** Visitors mostly see dogs and litters as
  rows on one page, with photos doing most of the work.
- **The facts breeders show for a dog** are consistent across sites: registered and call
  name, date of birth, weight or size, generation or breed mix, a short list of health
  results, parents, and a bio. Show-focused kennels add titles and scores; doodle kennels
  add generation and coat.
- **Status is shown in words, colours and badges:** "All puppies are spoken for",
  "Reservations currently paused", "Pregnancy confirmed", "4 puppies may still be
  available", red RESERVED, green Available, orange corner badges.
- **Dates go stale in public:** "Fall of 2024", "UPDATED 9/4/2026", "This site was last
  updated 05/06/22". The sites that show an "updated" date look current; the ones that
  don't often aren't.
- **Facts trapped in images** are common on inner pages: puppy posters with prices and
  dates, dog collages with names, payment-method posters, credential checklists.
- **Blank areas and leftovers** recur: unloaded widgets, empty placeholder boxes, long
  runs of empty colour at the end of a page.
- **More privacy slips:** a guardian family's surname and town under each mother ("loved
  by the Smith family in Peoria (Guardian Home)", Gardner Lane); reviewers' full names on
  one site.
